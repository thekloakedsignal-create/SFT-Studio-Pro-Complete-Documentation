Part Eight

Managing & Exporting Your Work

A studio that cannot find yesterday's render is a sketchbook, not a studio. SFT Studio Pro treats your output as a library: organized on disk, searchable in the gallery, and exportable in the formats your clients and platforms expect.

8.1  The Output Library

Every render is written to your output library the moment it finishes — there is no save-and-hope. The gallery and the folder are two views of the same files: delete in one and the file moves to your system trash in both.

8.2  Naming and Organization

Files are named automatically from their batch label and sequence number, and every image carries a sidecar record of its prompt, seed, and settings. The recommended discipline is simple:

  • Label every batch before you generate it.
  • Rate keepers with stars as you review (keys 1–5).
  • Filter the gallery by label, rating, or pipeline to isolate any deliverable in seconds.

8.3  Searching the Gallery

The gallery search field matches against prompts, labels, and pipeline names. Because prompts are indexed, a search for lighthouse surfaces every lighthouse frame you have ever generated — including the ones you forgot you made.

8.4  Exporting

Select any set of images and choose Export, or press Ctrl+Shift+E. The export dialog offers:

  • Format — PNG for lossless delivery, or JPG with quality control for the web.
  • Naming — keep library names, or apply a custom pattern with sequence numbering.
  • Color data — export the graded result as you see it, or the flat original render.
  • Metadata — optionally embed prompt and seed information in exported files.

Video clips export from the same dialog with the container and quality options appropriate to motion.

8.5  Backing Up

Your output library and project files are ordinary folders on disk — include them in your normal backup routine and your entire studio is protected. Moving to a new machine means moving the folders and reconnecting your provider key; nothing is locked to a single installation.

Tip. Seeds are only useful if they survive. Sidecar metadata and project files both preserve them — keep both, and no result is ever truly lost.
