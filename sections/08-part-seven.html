Part Seven

Professional Color Grading

Generation gives you the image; grading gives it the voice. SFT Studio Pro includes a genuine colorist's stage — the grading bay — where looks are built, saved, and applied across stills and clips with complete consistency.

7.1  The Grading Philosophy

Every grading operation in SFT Studio Pro is non-destructive. Grades are stored as instructions alongside your images, not baked into pixels, so you can return to any image months later, change one control, and export again — the original render is always underneath, untouched.

7.2  Opening the Grading Bay

Select an image or clip and choose Grade from the context menu, or press Ctrl+E (⌘+E). The grading bay opens with the selected work centred, the grade panel on the right, and a before/after wipe available with the \ key.

7.3  The Grade Panel, Section by Section

Exposure and Tone

Exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, and blacks. Establish the tonal skeleton here before touching color — almost every grading problem is, at heart, an exposure problem.

Color Temperature

White balance shift (cool to warm) and tint (green to magenta). Use this to correct the scene's neutral tone, then push it deliberately when you want the grade to feel warmer or colder than life.

Hue, Saturation, Luminance

Per-channel control across eight hue ranges. This is where you tame an aggressive sky, deepen foliage, or isolate a single accent color against a muted frame.

Split Toning

Independent color casts for shadows and highlights — the classic cinematic move. Cool shadows against warm highlights is the default look of modern film, and it lives here in two sliders.

Finishing

Vignette, grain, and sharpness. Grain in particular sells generated imagery: a whisper of it unifies the frame and removes the digital stillness that marks un-finished renders.

7.4  Pipelines and Presets

A pipeline is a saved, ordered stack of grading operations. Build a look once, save it as a preset, and it appears in the pipeline menu for one-click application to any image, batch, or clip. Pipelines can be:

  • Applied retrospectively — select a hundred finished images and apply a pipeline to all of them at once.
  • Chained to generation — attach a pipeline in the generation panel so every render arrives already graded.
  • Exported and shared — pipeline files travel between machines and collaborators unchanged.

Tip. A documented house look is a competitive advantage. Save your pipeline, name it after the project, and reuse it for the life of that body of work.

7.5  A Repeatable Grading Workflow

  1. Set exposure and contrast until the histogram breathes — no clipping unless it is a choice.
  2. Correct white balance to neutral, then bias it deliberately for mood.
  3. Shape individual hues in HSL; mute what should be quiet before you saturate what should sing.
  4. Add split toning in the last ten percent, subtly.
  5. Finish with vignette and grain, then judge the frame at fit-to-window, not zoomed in.
