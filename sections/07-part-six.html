Part Six

Text-to-Video (T2V)

SFT Studio Pro's T2V pipelines turn prompts — and the stills you have already approved — into short, finished motion. The same disciplines you learned for stills apply here, with motion added as a new creative dimension.

6.1  What T2V Produces

A T2V job renders a short video clip from a text prompt, rendered at 16:9 by default — the native frame for motion. Clips arrive in the gallery alongside your stills, marked with a video badge, and play in the viewer with standard transport controls.

6.2  Writing Prompts for Motion

A video prompt describes the scene the way a still prompt does, then adds how it moves. Describe one clear motion, and one camera behaviour at most:

  • Fog drifts across a harbour at dawn, slow push-in, gulls crossing frame left.
  • Rain on a neon shopfront at night, camera locked off, reflections shimmering.
  • A wildflower meadow in late light, gentle handheld sway, stems moving in the wind.

Tip. Conflicting motion instructions — fast dolly and locked off in the same prompt — produce jittery, indecisive clips. Commit to one movement and let it carry the shot.

6.3  Starting From a Still

The most reliable T2V workflow begins with a still you have already approved. Select any image in the gallery, choose Send to T2V, and the still becomes the first frame of the clip. Add a motion-only prompt — describing the movement, not the scene — and the render keeps your approved composition while bringing it to life. This is the pipeline to use for client work, where the frame has already been signed off.

6.4  T2V Settings

  • Duration controls clip length in seconds; longer clips take proportionally longer to render.
  • Motion strength scales how much the frame moves, from a barely-breathing still life to full kinetic scenes.
  • Seed behaves exactly as it does for stills: lock it to reproduce a clip, randomize it to explore.

6.5  Reviewing and Exporting Video

Finished clips can be graded in the same pipelines as stills — looks apply per frame across the whole clip. Export directly from the gallery or the inspector; the export dialog lists the available container and quality options for your installation. Video jobs render at priority normal by default and can be reordered in the queue like any other job.

Cost awareness. Video renders consume substantially more provider credits than stills. Previsualize with two-second clips at low motion strength before committing to final length.
