Part Five

Batch Generation & Seeds

Single images are for sketches; batches are for production. SFT Studio Pro's batch engine and its deterministic seeds are what turn a good prompt into a dependable body of work.

5.1  Creating a Batch

Set the image count in the generation panel to any value up to your batch limit and press Generate. The studio expands the job into individual queue cards — one per image — and renders them in sequence. You can keep working while the batch runs: draft the next prompt, grade finished images, or queue another batch behind the first.

5.2  Understanding Seeds

A seed is the number that initializes the randomness of a render. The same prompt, model detectors, ratio, and seed always produce the same image. This single property is the foundation of professional AI workflows, because it makes results repeatable.

  • Seed = -1 (the default) means a fresh random seed for every image.
  • Enter a specific number and the batch renders that exact composition.
  • Every image's seed is recorded in the inspector, forever.

5.3  The Variation Pattern

The most productive pattern in the studio is the prompt-batch-seed loop:

  1. Write a prompt and generate a batch of four to eight images with random seeds.
  2. Find the frame that is closest to what you wanted.
  3. Click Use Seed in the inspector to lock that composition.
  4. Refine the prompt — light, style, detail — and generate again on the same seed.

The composition holds while your refinements land. Two or three loops like this converge on results that feel authored rather than found.

5.4  Seed Sweeps

When you want to explore rather than refine, a seed sweep does the opposite: hold the prompt still and vary the seed. Set a fixed prompt, choose a count, and each image in the batch receives the next seed in sequence. Sweep seeds written down beside a prompt give you a map of that prompt's entire landscape — which compositions it can reach, and which it cannot.

5.5  Managing Large Batches

  • Pause the queue at any time; finished images are already in the gallery.
  • Cancel individual cards to keep the rest of the batch running.
  • Tag a batch with a label in the queue header so its images are grouped in the gallery and on disk.
  • Queue as many batches as you like — the studio renders strictly in order.

Tip. Batch labels become folder names during export. Name them as you would name client deliverables, and your filing is done before you begin.
